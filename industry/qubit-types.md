# Qubit types

Each technology trades off speed, error rates, and how easily it scales.

| Type | How it works | Strengths | Challenges | Companies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Superconducting** | Tiny circuits cooled near absolute zero behave like artificial atoms | Fast gates; built with chip-making techniques | Needs dilution refrigerators; qubits mostly talk only to neighbors; short coherence times | IBM, Google, Rigetti, AWS, Alice & Bob |
| **Trapped ions** | Charged atoms held in place by electric fields, controlled with lasers | Very low error rates; any qubit can interact with any other | Slower gates; scaling to many ions is hard | Quantinuum, IonQ |
| **Neutral atoms** | Uncharged atoms held by focused laser beams ("optical tweezers") | Large arrays; atoms can be rearranged | Atoms can be lost; gate speeds and fidelities still improving | QuEra, Pasqal, Atom Computing, Infleqtion |
| **Photonic** | Particles of light carry the information | Works at or near room temperature for parts of the system; fits with fiber networks | Photons are hard to make interact; photon loss | PsiQuantum, Xanadu |
| **Spin qubits** | The spin of single electrons in silicon | Tiny; could reuse existing chip factories | Hard to make qubits uniform | Intel |
| **Topological** | Information stored in exotic states of matter that resist errors | Could need much less error correction | Still being demonstrated; results have been debated | Microsoft |
| **Quantum annealing** | Superconducting qubits that settle into a low-energy answer | Thousands of qubits today, good for some optimization | Not a general-purpose quantum computer | D-Wave |
